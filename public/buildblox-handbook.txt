14  PLAYER HANDBOOK
===================
14.1  START A WORLD

From the title screen, choose New World, enter a name, and select Survival
or Building. The seed is an unsigned integer of up to 64 bits. Leave the
seed blank to let the app generate one and show it on the world information
screen. Survival starts with an empty belt and pack. Building starts with
the material palette available.

The spawn setup guarantees these resources within 64 blocks: six loose
slate rubble, four alder logs accessible at ground level, at least four
brush fiber, two reed fiber, one dry sheltered site, and a patch of loam.
It also guarantees an accessible cave entrance or quarry within 160
blocks. A starter quarry includes at least eight obtainable basalt
rubble and deposits of tin and copper. This guarantees the tool and
workshop route on any supported seed. The generator checks a fixed,
coordinate-sorted ring of walkable starting sites around the origin.
If none meet these constraints, stamp a starter clearing and quarry
route with a reserved feature priority before decorative features.
Columns that intersect it replay the same stamped geometry from its
global anchor. Landmarks outside the starter region are selected
through the regular generator.

14.2  YOUR FIRST SHELTER AND TOOLS

1. Walk through loose slate rubble to collect it. Break a brush and a reed
   to collect both types of fiber. Hand-break alder logs, which have a
   lower harvest requirement than structural rock.
2. Open the pack with Tab. In Quick Assembly, make cord, then a stone
   hammer. Select that hammer and hold the mining input on slate. The
   progress arc tracks continuous effort on the selected face. Clear
   enough rubble and logs for a workbench and building materials.
3. Place the workbench on dry ground. Convert a log to three dry planks,
   and use more planks to make timber panels. Place floor and wall panels
   from the visible face of a supporting block. Use a doorway and a
   roof. Leave two blocks of vertical clearance on walking routes.
4. Craft a camp cot for rest and a storage drawer for stock. Place the
   cot with one clear exit tile on either side and enough ceiling space.
   Shelter is evaluated at the cot from rain exposure, ground support,
   clearance and nearby damaging fluids. Rest refills stamina and warmth
   in valid shelter. The primary respawn mechanism is the marker stand.
5. Make a field hoe. Till loam, plant grain seed, and aim a water flask
   at the bed if it is dry. Level 2 hydration and light 8 are sufficient.
   Harvest mature grain and keep the seed source in the field.

Starter shelter example. Find a flat five-by-five footprint. Use its
center for the floor coordinate origin. Place 25 floor panels, then
two wall courses around the 16-cell perimeter. Omit two vertically
aligned wall panels at the midpoint of one side for the entrance.
Cap the room with 25 roof panels at Y=3 relative to the floor.
The floor, walls and roof use 80 panels, which take 80 dry planks
or 27 logs with one plank left over. Keep the center clear to walk
between the cot, workbench and drawer. Put the stove on a fire-safe
masonry cell and leave its front accessible.

14.3  OPEN A WORKSHOP

Use the stone hammer to recover tin and copper in a cave or quarry. Gather
red clay with a trowel or by hand. Go to the workbench and create a kiln.
Use four clay lumps to fire two clay units, and gather four basalt bricks
from the workbench to make a smelter. For the first charcoal job, put an
extra raw log in the kiln's fuel slot and a separate log in its input.
Collect the charcoal and move part of it to the smelter. Tin enables basic
mechanisms, copper enables the metal hammer and wiring, and iron and quartz
extend the route into deeper construction. Make extra fuel before entering
the thermal hollows, where useful equipment and weather protection matter.

For a first powered build, place one switch, place adjacent wire on
supported surfaces, and place a lamp with its input terminal facing
the end of the wire. Toggle the switch with Use. Open the device inspector
to see the high input arrive. Add a relay if the path must move up a wall.
Add a timer after the switch to make a lamp flash while the switch is on.
Fit a water wheel to a channel with circulating or replenished flow when
the build calls for a steady signal from moving water.

14.4  EXPLORE, TRAVEL, AND RECOVER

Surface weather and depth drive equipment choices. Equip a rain cape for
long wetland trips, a coat for high peaks, a lamp for caves, a flask for
watering or stocking fish, and some cooked food for healing. Cold areas
drain warmth gradually, and wind ridges have steep falls even in clear
weather. Refill a flask from a full source with a scoop. Place a rope line
from a supported anchor to reach lower passages. A hand glider requires
the equipment slot and open air before activation. A skiff requires enough
water under its hull and an unobstructed dismount space beside it.

Set a marker stand next to the workshop and choose Set Recovery Point.
The marker panel shows its bearing, distance, access settings, and
whether a safe respawn tile is available. On recovery, the belt, worn
clothing, and equipped tool return with the player. The loose pack goes
into the owner crate. Follow its temporary compass marker and select
Recover to transfer as many stacks as will fit. The failure screen
provides the last cause of damage, return location, and crate position.
If the registered marker becomes unusable, spawn at the next valid
owner marker, then at the world origin camp.

14.5  BUILDING MODE AND PROJECT TOOLS

The build palette groups structural materials, decorations, plants, and
devices. Scroll to cycle palettes and use Alt to pick a material or
variant. Flight uses Jump to ascend and Crouch to descend. Paint applies
a supported cosmetic variant to the selected material. Fill starts with
two corner selections, displays its bounds and cell count, and commits
as one transaction. Use the same selection for replacement fills.

The build preview colors invalid cells and names the blocking condition.
A denied bulk edit modifies zero cells. The inspector reports the block ID,
facing, connection mask, and block and fluid levels. If a lamp stays off,
check the source, terminal facing, wire connectivity, and
whether the intended segment is loaded. If a pump stays idle, check fluid
at its intake, capacity at its output, and power at its input.

15  BLOCK AND ITEM ENCYCLOPEDIA
=============================
The identifiers in this chapter are searchable in the inventory or build
palette where that form exists. The numeric mining work and tier table is
in Chapter 06. A generated-only state is created by terrain generation,
simulation, or another action. For crafted blocks, recipes are in 07.
Station drop entries assume the station is idle and empty.

15.1  EVERY BLOCK

BLOCK air
  Found in open space. Carries up to eight fluid units and lets light
  pass. Clearing a regular cell changes the solid layer to air. The
  selection ray continues through empty cells.
BLOCK foundation
  The bottom two layers at Y=-96 and -95. The world cannot be excavated
  below this layer. Foundation is excluded from the Survival item list
  and normal editing commands.
BLOCK loam
  Common meadow and woodland topsoil. Place as a full cube, or use a
  field hoe on its upper face to convert it into a plant_bed. Break by
  hand, hammer, or trowel to recover loam.
BLOCK peat
  Wetland and cold-forest soil. Extends the hydration timer of planting
  beds within one block of its upper surface by 30 seconds. Place as a
  full cube. Recover with a trowel or by hand.
BLOCK sand
  Coastal dunes and basins. Falls when unsupported, becoming a falling
  block entity until landing on a valid surface. Harvest with a trowel
  and use in the kiln to make glass_sheet.
BLOCK gravel
  Bay floors and stony terrain. Falls under gravity like sand. Safe
  excavation uses short supported rows. Loose starter rubble can appear
  separately on the surface without mining the gravel.
BLOCK red_clay
  Wetland banks and dry scrub patches. Break to collect one clay_lump.
  A trowel shortens harvest. Place a clay_lump against a valid supporting
  face to restore a red_clay block, or reserve it for the kiln.
BLOCK snow_crust
  A thin layer formed by local snowfall and cold surfaces. Gives an
  audible crunch and provides full footing on the surface underneath.
  Warmth can melt the layer into available fluid capacity. Harvest or
  palette placement uses the thin layer as a block item.
BLOCK salt_crust
  Thin deposit on exposed qualifying brine margins in bays and basins.
  Harvest drops one salt_flake. A scraped surface can regrow crust
  after one tide transition if still suitable and unoccupied.
BLOCK slate
  Structural native rock at middle and upper elevations. A qualifying
  stone hammer yields slate_rubble. Slate brick is its placeable
  survival building counterpart. Building palette can place native slate.
BLOCK basalt
  Dark native rock in deep terrain and exposed hot shelves. A qualifying
  stone hammer yields basalt_rubble for durable construction and the kiln.
  The plain block is a building-palette material.
BLOCK ore_tin
  Light speckles in the qualifying altitude band. Mine with a stone
  hammer or better to collect one raw_tin. Foundational ore for the
  trowel, hand saw, and bearing recipes.
BLOCK ore_copper
  Rust and teal markings in eligible rock. Mine with a stone hammer or
  better for one raw_copper. Smelt for the metal hammer and wiring.
BLOCK ore_iron
  Pale bands in deeper eligible rock. A metal hammer or better drops
  one raw_iron. Carry a verified T2 tool before opening a visible vein.
BLOCK quartz_cluster
  A luminous growth on exposed rock in a crystal fault. Supported by
  its attached face. A T2 or T3 hammer gives one glow_quartz. Removing
  the support detaches it and produces the same drop if the support
  removal had a qualifying tool tier.
BLOCK alder_log
  Alder trunk. Places with axis following the selected face. An exposed
  natural trunk can be tapped with a brush knife once per day for one
  amber_resin. The item supplies dry planks and early recipes.
BLOCK pine_log
  Tall conifer trunk. Axis follows placement face. Use a saw for quick
  harvesting. The dry planks preserve the pine palette and grain.
BLOCK foliage
  Thin cutout canopy, tagged as alder or pine. Harvest gives a matching
  seed on 18% of adult foliage rolls. A disconnected crown begins decay
  after 30 active seconds and rolls the same deterministic seed chance.
BLOCK brush
  Low scrub and tall dry stems in suitable light. Harvest gives one
  brush_fiber. Using a brush knife clips it to a regrowing base; ordinary
  breaking removes the whole plant.
BLOCK reed
  Wetland stalk. Harvest the upper section with a brush knife for one
  reed_fiber and preserve the base. Plant a fiber in damp clay or peat
  beside shallow water to start another stalk.
BLOCK kelp
  Underwater plant on gravel or sand with fill ≥6. Harvest fronds from
  the top and leave the base. Plant one frond on submerged gravel or
  sand to start new kelp.
BLOCK fungus
  Found on moist cave surfaces. A cap can be planted on peat or clay
  at light 2–7. A brush knife harvests a cap and leaves the growth base.
  Ordinary breaking yields a cap and clears the spot.
BLOCK grain_crop
  Formed by planting grain_seed on a plant_bed. Mature at age 4.
  An ordinary mature harvest gives two grain and one seed. A tool
  harvest gives two grain and regrows the plant from age 1.
BLOCK plant_bed
  Created by hoeing loam. Stores hydration and the last growth update.
  Placement is through tilling. An ordinary block break drops loam.
BLOCK timber_panel
  Full construction block, with alder or pine variant selected by the
  planks used. Cutaway angles and orientations are cosmetic. Recover
  a panel intact with a saw or hammer.
BLOCK slate_brick
  Full grey masonry cube. Place as a wall, support, floor, or finished
  material. Convert to matching stairs or half steps at a workbench.
BLOCK basalt_brick
  Full dark masonry cube with higher mining work. Its heat-resistant
  surfaces suit workshop and vent builds. Convert to matching stairs
  or half steps at a workbench.
BLOCK clay_tile
  Full fired building block. Bake a salt-and-colorant glaze to change
  the visual style. Inspect the item to see its glaze before placement.
BLOCK glass_panel
  Thin transparent barrier with directional facing and a solid plane.
  Receives and transmits light. A hammer recovers the panel at the
  listed mining work. Unbroken panels support neighboring frames.
BLOCK half_step
  Lower or upper half cube chosen by the aimed face height. Places as
  slate or basalt masonry. Opposite compatible half steps combine into
  a full matching brick block.
BLOCK stair_step
  Two joined collision cuboids for elevation changes. Stores facing and
  upright or inverted placement. A side connection affects appearance.
  The simulation's step assist handles its half-block increments.
BLOCK fence_section
  Narrow barrier that connects to compatible adjacent blocks. Its four
  horizontal connection bits update when a neighbor changes. Physics
  collision extends above the rendered rail to 1.35 blocks.
BLOCK gate
  Hinged opening in a path or fence. Use toggles it manually. A high
  signal on its rear terminal requests an open state and a low signal
  releases the override. It waits to close if occupied.
BLOCK ladder
  A wall-attached thin climbable volume. Place on a solid face and
  stack vertically to extend it. A removed support causes the ladder
  to drop as an item.
BLOCK anchor
  An attachment point for rope and boat tethering. Requires a supporting
  solid face. Valid placement reserves one rope or skiff connection.
  Removing the anchor releases its attachments, with the rope dropping
  at its home cell.
BLOCK marker_stand
  Created from a survey kit or found at landmarks. Name it, choose
  visibility, and register it as a recovery point when the surrounding
  standing space is valid. Built stands can be moved after clearing
  their registered player marker entries.
BLOCK camp_cot
  A two-cell rest item with facing. The supported foot and head cells
  must both be clear at placement. Rest restores warmth and stamina
  in shelter. Either half breaks the pair and returns one cot.
BLOCK workbench
  Opens assembly recipes. Pulls checked amounts from the acting
  player's belt and pack. Output requires free space. The bench itself
  is stateless between transactions.
BLOCK kiln
  Heat station for clay, glass, quicklime, charcoal and glaze. Put fuel
  and the required inputs in their assigned slots and queue a recipe.
  An incomplete active job can be cancelled to recover its reserved inputs.
BLOCK smelter
  Converts individual raw metal units to ingots. Accepts charcoal.
  If output is full, a finished job waits until collected.
BLOCK stove
  Cooks food and provides heat once fueled. Burns charcoal or oil.
  Workshop status displays remaining heat and current recipe progress.
BLOCK storage_drawer
  A 24-slot placeable inventory. Use Interact to open the drawer.
  Empty it before breaking to recover its block item.
BLOCK recovery_crate
  Created by a player's defeat. The owner can recover items until expiry
  or until empty. A crate occupying a blocked space is relocated to a
  valid nearby tile by the recovery system.
BLOCK lamp
  A quartz-based light with its rear input marked. At high input it
  emits RGB levels (15,13,8). At low input it emits zero. Changing the
  setting queues a local light rebuild.
BLOCK switch
  Wall or floor control. Use toggles its fixed output and visibly updates
  the control plate. Wire joins the terminal side rather than the handle.
BLOCK wire
  A supported signal connection. Orient to adjoining wires and device
  terminals. Paint marks separate visual circuits; only a physical break
  or relay isolates them.
BLOCK relay
  Accepts input on the back and drives the front. Moves signals between
  levels when wall-mounted. Inspection configures delay and inversion.
BLOCK timer
  On an enabled input, advances its tick counter and emits one-tick
  pulses at the chosen interval. An off input resets the timer.
BLOCK sensor
  Reads the cell or volume in front, and reports a predicate on its
  output side. Available readings include fluid depth, local light,
  occupied space, and container fullness.
BLOCK pump
  Pulls fluid from one face and transfers up to two units to the opposite
  face each active fluid step. Stops at saturation, no fluid, denied
  space, or absent power. An ordinary hammer recovers the empty device.
BLOCK water_wheel
  Set its driven face alongside moving fluid. Its output stays high
  once flow meets the threshold for three fluid steps. Check that the
  output terminal is outside the water channel.
BLOCK sluice
  A directional fluid gate. When closed it blocks transfer through its
  controlled faces. When open it passes flow without creating volume.
  Use its rear terminal or local control to change state.
BLOCK lift_platform
  A moving standing surface constrained to a vertical guide of anchors
  and wall faces. A high input requests upper docking. A low input
  requests lower docking. The platform pauses if the path becomes blocked.
BLOCK geothermal_grate
  Rare fixed landmark in thermal terrain. Periodically vents heat and
  warns nearby creatures and players with particles and sound. With
  T3 mining, it returns two basalt_rubble and one glow_quartz.

15.2  EVERY NON-BLOCK INVENTORY TYPE

Stack limits. Harvested ingredients stack to 64. Processed ingredients,
projectiles, and placeable building items stack to 48, except unique
device configurations and storage with payload, which each take one slot.
Food stacks to 16. Tools, worn equipment, the skiff, and flasks containing
fluids or fish take one slot. Empty flasks stack to 16. Split a stack to
consume one item when an interaction creates a single new payload item.

ITEM slate_rubble
  Pick up on starter ground or mine slate with T1. Make a starter
  hammer, pellets, workbench parts and slate bricks.
ITEM basalt_rubble
  Mine basalt with T1 or recover from a starter quarry. Craft dark
  masonry or burn it in a kiln to make quicklime.
ITEM clay_lump
  Dig red clay. Replace as red_clay, fire into clay material, or use
  as a colorant for ochre and red glazes.
ITEM brush_fiber
  Clip or break brush. Also shed by a dune runner. Ingredient in cord.
ITEM reed_fiber
  Harvest wetland stalks. Use as a cord and insulation ingredient, or
  plant in a suitable wetland cell to regrow a stalk.
ITEM kelp_frond
  Clip underwater kelp. Replant on suitable submerged bed, cook as
  stew, or process into oil and colorants.
ITEM fungus_cap
  Harvest on moist cave ground or plant on suitable peat or clay.
  Roast in a stove, combine in kelp stew, or use for plum glaze.
ITEM salt_flake
  Scrape salt crust at the coast or in a basin. Use in glazing, oil,
  stew and salted eggs.
ITEM grain
  Harvest mature crops. Use in flatbread, feeding, and creature
  bonding. It is separate from the planted seed item.
ITEM grain_seed
  Find on wild mature grain or recover from cultivated crops. Use on
  the exposed surface of a plant bed.
ITEM alder_seed
  Collect from mature alder foliage or dropped by an alder glider.
  Plant in moist, clear loam to grow alder wood and resin sources.
ITEM pine_seed
  Collect from adult pine foliage. Plant in cold or elevated terrain
  with clear vertical growth space.
ITEM raw_tin
  Obtain from properly mined tin ore and smelt into tin_ingot.
ITEM raw_copper
  Obtain from properly mined copper ore and smelt into copper_ingot.
ITEM raw_iron
  Obtain with T2 mining or by a rare slate stalker drop. Smelt into
  iron_ingot.
ITEM glow_quartz
  Harvest quartz clusters with T2 mining. Required for lamps, sensors,
  and survey kits.
ITEM amber_resin
  Tap a suitable natural alder trunk with a brush knife or defeat
  husk beetles. Used for insulation glue, wiring, flasks, glaze and skiffs.
ITEM hide
  Salvage natural cliff grazer shedding, or receive as creature loot.
  Fit clothing or a glider at a workbench.
ITEM egg
  Collect from bonded marsh hens' nests. Cook with salt for healing.
ITEM tide_shell
  Obtain from shed or harvested shellbacks. Use directly on a finished
  timber panel for shell inlay, consuming one per panel.
ITEM cord
  Quick-assemble from brush and reed fiber. Used in ropes, tools,
  transport, construction and clothing.
ITEM charcoal
  Fire a log in a kiln. Fuel for kiln, smelter and stove. Also a
  pigment input. Fuel burn time is 20 simulated seconds per unit.
ITEM fired_clay
  Fire clay lumps at a kiln. Used in tile, melting equipment, scoops,
  and empty flasks.
ITEM quicklime
  Process basalt rubble in a kiln. Used in glass and light glazes.
ITEM tin_ingot
  Smelt raw tin. Used in saws, trowels and bearing assemblies.
ITEM copper_ingot
  Smelt raw copper. Used in metal tools, signals, lamps, bearings,
  survey instruments and selected glazes.
ITEM iron_ingot
  Smelt raw iron. Used in reinforced hammers, gears, pumps, lifts,
  sluices and rope anchors.
ITEM copper_spool
  Assemble copper with resin. Make wire segments or use it as an
  input for device assemblies.
ITEM gear
  Assemble iron and a bearing. Transfers motion in devices and is an
  ingredient in timers, pumps, sluices, wheels and lifts.
ITEM bearing
  Combine tin and copper. Component of a gear, switched devices,
  pumps, relays and gates.
ITEM glass_sheet
  Fire sand and quicklime in a kiln. Cut into panels or assemble
  into an enclosed quartz lamp.
ITEM dry_plank
  Cut wood into usable construction material. Variants keep their
  source species. Used for structures, stations, machines and the skiff.
ITEM oil
  Process kelp and salt. Supplies 25 seconds of stove fuel.
ITEM survey_kit
  Craft with copper, quartz and one dry plank. Install in a marker
  stand to place a named navigation and recovery point.
ITEM sling_pellet
  Quick-assemble four from one slate rubble. Consumed on an accepted
  shot even when the projectile misses.
ITEM flatbread
  Cook two grain. Eat to restore up to 12 health.
ITEM kelp_stew
  Cook kelp, fungus and salt. Eat to restore up to 25 health.
ITEM roasted_fungus
  Cook one cap. Eat to restore up to eight health.
ITEM salted_egg
  Cook one egg and one salt flake. Eat to restore up to 15 health.
ITEM stone_hammer
  Starter T1 tool for slate, basalt, tin and copper. Has 100 durability
  and mining speed 1.6. Also handles basic construction recovery.
ITEM metal_hammer
  T2 copper tool with 230 durability and mining speed 2.4. Unlocks
  iron ore and quartz clusters.
ITEM reinforced_hammer
  T3 iron-and-basalt tool with 450 durability and mining speed 3.2.
  Unlocks full salvage from geothermal grates.
ITEM hand_saw
  Tin blade with 160 durability and 2.7 mining speed on wood. Recovers
  compatible panels and logs while preserving species.
ITEM trowel
  Tin tool with 140 durability and 2.4 mining speed on soft soil.
  Used to collect clay, sand, loam and peat.
ITEM brush_knife
  Starter cutter with 100 durability. Clips plants without destroying
  the rooted cell and taps productive natural alder trunks.
ITEM field_hoe
  Starter cultivating tool with 120 durability. Converts loam into a
  plant bed and can restore planting state after clearing a crop.
ITEM scoop
  Clay-lined tool with 120 durability. Transfers fluid to or from a
  flask. A successful eight-unit transfer consumes one durability.
ITEM sling
  Ranged tool with 160 durability. Consume one pellet and one tool
  use per accepted shot. Useful against moving cave threats.
ITEM rain_cape
  Worn clothing item. Halves rain exposure warmth loss. Select in the
  clothing slot before long coastal or fen travel.
ITEM insulated_coat
  Worn clothing item. Halves cold exposure warmth loss. Use in frostwood
  or high snowy terrain. Only one clothing item is active at a time.
ITEM rope_line
  One portable line up to 12 blocks long. Use against an anchored
  exposed face with open air below. Remove through the anchor or top cell.
ITEM hand_glider
  Equipped item for a controlled descent in open air. Start with
  300 durability and spend one point per second of sustained glide.
ITEM empty_flask
  Make from fired clay and resin. With a scoop selected, hold at least
  one flask in belt or pack, then collect eight units of water or brine.
ITEM water_flask
  Starts with eight fresh-water units. Use with a scoop to deposit its
  remaining amount in available capacity, or water four dry plant beds
  with two units each. The stack state tracks any remaining units.
ITEM brine_flask
  Carries up to eight brine units. Deposit into a basin or move to
  an eligible channel. Crops respond to fresh water.
ITEM fish_flask
  Created by using an eligible water flask on a shoal darter. Holds
  one fish. Release into a submerged valid cell and receive empty_flask.
ITEM skiff
  A wooden two-seat vehicle with 12 slots of cargo. Deploy with
  sufficient fill and hull clearance. Clean out cargo and release tethers
  before recovering it as an inventory item.

16  WORLD AND CREATURE ENCYCLOPEDIA
==================================
16.1  BIOME GUIDE

The surface predicates and cave selection order are in 03. Each entry
below adds identifying terrain, functional resources, and local hazards.
Use the current biome in Map or the developer cell inspector to find out
which biome owns a border cell.

BIOME brine_bay
  Deep shoreline water over gravel and kelp. Follow the high-water line
  for salt crust and shellbacks. Shoal darters use full water near gravel
  cover. Mark tidal reservoir cells for mills and plan hull clearance
  across the full tide cycle. Release fish where their body fits in
  water and adjoining cells have eight fluid units of spare capacity.
BIOME storm_coast
  Sandy beaches, marsh rushes, dune slopes and tidal channels. Find
  brush fiber, reeds, dune runners and shellbacks. Tide mills can use
  a flowing coast channel. Engineer deep foundations under sand so
  unsupported excavations stay stable. Sheltered coves favor skiffs.
BIOME frost_crown
  High cold peaks with open slate and snow crust. Cliff grazers shelter
  on broad terraces. Snow crust and freezing precipitation make
  insulation valuable. Summit paths need flat landings and guardrails.
  A high marker stand provides a visible return bearing.
BIOME cinder_shelf
  Raised dark basalt terraces with hot fissures and low moisture.
  Exposed basalt provides an early supply of durable masonry when
  available nearby. Trace compact fissures to collapsed quarry routes.
  Deeper vents generate close-range heat damage during eruptions.
BIOME wind_ridge
  Elevated slate crests with sparse pine and wide views. Search ledges
  for pine material, cliff grazers and survey landmarks. Carry climbing
  gear or bring the glider to make a controlled return down long slopes.
BIOME lantern_fen
  Low wetland with clay banks, teal peat, pools and golden reeds.
  Marsh hens and lantern moths use the vegetation edges. Clay and fiber
  make this a useful first workshop region. Fog occurs frequently.
  Use raised paths, shallow channels and a marked home approach.
BIOME frostwood
  Cold pine stands above dark peat and patches of snow. Pine seed and
  logs are useful building supplies. Look for sheltered routes between
  stands and for grazers on open edges. Cold exposure remains active
  under clear skies at night.
BIOME salt_basin
  Low-moisture flats, salt crust and shallow occasional brine. The wide
  floor is easy to survey for construction. Source steady fresh water
  before planting. Use basin salt for glazing, food and lamp-workshop
  decoration materials.
BIOME red_scrub
  Warm dry brush on rust clay and sand. Good source of brush fiber,
  clay and amber resin from beetles. Stay near water on long trips.
  Husk beetles guard resin areas and signal a charge with a pause and
  wing rattle.
BIOME rainwood
  Dense alder groves, leafy cutout canopies, moist loam and fungus.
  Tap mature natural trunks for renewable resin. Alder gliders move
  between canopies. Root vaults can occur below the trees. Bring a lamp
  to investigate shadowed hollows and navigation landmarks.
BIOME silt_meadow
  Open loam flats and low hills with grain, brush and scattered alder.
  Easy access to agriculture and building ground. Gather wild grain seed
  and find nearby stone for starter construction. Suitable open clearings
  can contain survey stations.
BIOME thermal_hollows
  Deep caves with hot basalt, steam and vents. Ember crawlers gather
  near a heated route. A steam surge applies heat in a visible column.
  Move across a vent during its quiet phase, or bridge around it.
  Bring a reinforced hammer for grate salvage.
BIOME crystal_fault
  Inset cavities and illuminated seams containing glow quartz clusters.
  Use a metal hammer for full harvest. Follow lit faces to spot chambers
  and possible observatories. Some adjoining dark tunnels support
  slate stalkers. Portable light makes the transition easier to read.
BIOME root_vault
  Shallow chambers below wetland or woodland with hanging roots,
  fungus and seep water. Cave slugs and dusk moths can occupy its
  margins. A suitable cap and a lamp with controlled placement can
  turn one chamber into a reliable harvest route.
BIOME slate_galleries
  General underground tunnels between specialized cave types.
  Look for raw tin and copper in their height bands and iron deeper
  down. Low-light branches can contain slate stalkers. Mark route
  junctions, raise the floor above water, and close abandoned shafts.

16.2  LANDMARKS, STOCK, AND REWARDS

Landmark definitions declare allowed biomes, a bounding box, stock table,
and access tests. The seed chooses positions, facing and stock. Use the
following default families and one deterministic optional stock roll each.
Generated stocks are written into the world when their door or drawer
first opens. Reopening or reloading uses the stored inventory.

LANDMARK survey_station
  Biomes silt_meadow, wind_ridge, frostwood, rainwood or red_scrub.
  A 7×7 roofed crossing with a stand and one 24-slot drawer. Default
  stock is two cord and two dry planks. Optional route cache chooses
  one of six slate rubble, one water flask, or one survey kit with
  respective relative weights 5, 3 and 1.
LANDMARK tide_mill
  Biomes storm_coast or lantern_fen, adjoining active water. A 9×7

Items on ordinary terrain despawn after 36,000 active ticks. Recovery
crates have their own longer lifetime in 05. A failed pickup keeps its
item entity. Ownership delays exclude parties without access for the
specified ticks, then the stack can be collected by any player allowed
to pick up items there. A broken tool is consumed after its final use,
and the completing action still produces its normal output.

With a mining work value expressed in seconds, work per accepted held
tick is tool_speed / 30. Bare hands have speed 1.0 on permitted starter
materials, and 0.45 on standard rock. Preserve fractional progress on
consecutive packets for the same active target. Reset it after switching
tools or selecting another target. Tool speeds apply within their
preferred material families.

At 30 Hz, one 24-minute day is 43,200 ticks. For the first release, use
day fractions 0.00–0.10 for sunrise, 0.10–0.55 for full daylight,
0.55–0.65 for sunset, and 0.65–1.00 for night. Interpolate the sky
factor between 0.20 and 1.00 on sunrise and sunset. A gameplay light
query returns the larger of scaled sky light rounded down and the
brightest artificial channel. This feeds both crop checks and the
brightness conditions of creatures. A lamp can keep a grain bed
eligible through night if its propagated light is sufficient.

Plant growth occurs on a usable bed even when a player is away, as
long as the relevant simulation area is active. Save the next scheduled
growth tick and pause progress for inactive columns. Reloading takes
the saved age and active time. Run subsequent rolls on the active
schedule beginning from the restored state. Weather and tide use world
time, so exposed water and terrain reconcile toward the current phase
when loaded.

18.3  STATION AND TRANSPORT SETTINGS

A kiln, smelter and stove each have four input slots, one fuel slot,
two output slots, and a maximum of four queued jobs, including the
active job. A reservation records required inputs and owner. At
execution, fuel ignition spends one unit. Every simulated tick removes
one burn tick while cooking or heating. A completed job enters an
output-waiting state until its stack can be transferred. Break is
allowed after queued inputs, fuel, and output have been cleared.

The skiff targets 5.2 units/sec forward and 2.0 in reverse, with
2.5 units/sec² of acceleration. It turns up to 75 degrees/sec while
moving. Movement stalls when the four hull samples average under four
fluid units. The glider accelerates toward forward speed 8 units/sec
while active and limits falling speed to 2.5 units/sec in level air.
Steering rotates the pitch and yaw gradually. It closes on ground
contact or when the player runs out of durability. A sling pellet
launches from the hand toward the validated aim at 23 units/sec,
follows gravity, and resolves the first hit along each swept step.

18.4  DEVICE BUILD EXAMPLES

Switched wall lamp. Put a switch on a supported surface. Connect its
marked output to wire and then to the lamp's rear terminal. Set input
and output faces in the inspector if the route turns through a wall.
Toggle once. The lamp should light within two simulation ticks of the
signal reaching its wire network.

Demand-controlled watering. Place a pump between a fresh-water supply
and a channel next to planted beds. Put a sensor facing the destination
cell, set it to depth at least 6, and pass its output into an inverting
relay. Relay high when the channel is shallow. Set it to a 3-tick delay
and route its output to the pump. One ordinary fluid step fills up to
two requested units. Test the channel at empty and full.

Water-powered landing. Route a steady coastal channel across the driven
face of a wheel. At least one accepted unit along the driven face
counts as flow during that fluid step. Keep the terminal on dry ground. Wire the wheel
output to the landing lamps and place an anchor within reach of the
skiff. At low tide, verify at least four average fluid units under the
hull. Use a ramp or marked steps from the landing back toward the road.

Guided lift. Pick two stops on a vertical wall. Mount anchors in a
continuous eight-block-or-shorter guide, place the platform at the lower
stop, and wire a toggle to its input. The platform takes one block per
second along clear guide cells, carrying grounded passengers along.
If support is removed or a new block occupies its route, the platform
pauses and waits for a clear path or owner reset.
